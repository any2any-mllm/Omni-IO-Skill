Three sea-surface temperature anomaly maps show an ocean divided into regional stories. In map one, the North Pacific carries the clearest contrast: unusually warm water in the northeast, beside a cool central pool. By map two, that cool pool spreads westward, while the northeast warm patch remains. In map three, warmth becomes more continuous along the Arctic rim, and Southern Hemisphere warm bands persist. Red and blue show departures from a reference average, not absolute temperature. So these maps do not, by themselves, identify a named climate event or a long-term trend. They reveal a moving patchwork shaped by winds, currents, and ocean heat exchange — regional changes that matter together.
